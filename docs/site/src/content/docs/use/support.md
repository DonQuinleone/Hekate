Help is behind the **?** beside the bell. It always leads to this documentation; where the people who run your instance answer support, it also leads to your tickets and to a new one.

## Before you ask

### A file will not play

- **Still preparing.** A file plays once its stream is made. Large videos take a while; the file's row says how far it has got. Nothing needs doing.
- **Preparing failed.** The row says so, with an error reference such as `HK-7F3A-2C`. Uploading the file again, exported once more from the program it came from, fixes most damaged files. If it fails again, ask, quoting the reference.
- **A score or a document with a blank page.** A damaged PDF can show some pages and not others. Export it again from the engraving or layout program.
- **No sound.** Check the player's volume and the browser tab's; a guest's browser may also block sound until they click the page once.

### Reviewers cannot see a file

- **Members** see a project's files when they have a role in it. Check the project's People page.
- **Guests** see only what the review they were asked into holds, and only while it is open and their link is good. Send them a new link from the review; it withdraws the last.
- **Groups** asked into a review let in whoever is in the group when they open it.
- A review can override what its reviewers may do (download, comment, see others' comments) without changing the project.

### Loudness reads differently elsewhere

Hekate measures integrated loudness and true peak by the ITU and EBU method, over the whole file, against the project's delivery profile (Spotify, Apple Music, broadcast or your own). Another meter may read a short-term or momentary value, a different gating, or sample peak rather than true peak, and so differ by a decibel or so. Check which profile the project or track uses before reading too much into a difference.

## Tickets

With tickets on, your workspace's settings have a **Support** section, also reached from the help menu (**My tickets**, **Contact support**).

- **Anyone in the workspace can ask.** You see your own tickets; owners and admins see every ticket the workspace sent.
- **New ticket** shows the documentation, common answers and the status page first (with anything down for everyone named there, so you need not ask), then the form: a subject, a type (something is broken, a question, billing, an idea), a severity, the message and up to five attachments of 10 MB each.
- **Severity** says how much is stopped: Low and Normal on every plan; High, Urgent and Emergency by plan, where the instance charges. The form says which plan unlocks the rest.
- **Error references.** If something failed for you in the last day, the form offers to attach its reference, and any reference you quote in the message is attached too. The operators then see the whole story; you see only that it was attached.
- **The file or review in question.** Contact support from a file or review, and you can let the operators open it to look.
- **The reference**, such as `T-7F3A-2C`, is made when you send. You hear of an answer in your inbox and by email (at once or in your digest, as your notification settings say), and the thread lives in Support: reply there, or mark it resolved. A reply to a resolved ticket opens it again.

Where the instance answers somewhere else, its desk or mailbox is linked from the help menu and from Support instead.

## Status

**Status** in the help menu opens the instance's status page, readable without signing in: each service, its last 90 days, and anything its operators have posted as down for everyone, with their updates until it is resolved. While something is, the help menu's button carries a dot.
